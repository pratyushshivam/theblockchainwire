None of these are about blockchain. They are about the same habit, though: taking a system that is usually explained with a diagram and making it something you can hold. All five are open source, run entirely in the browser, and need no account.

## Latency

A three minute game about the speed of light. You place data centres and lay cables on a globe so every city gets an answer in under 100 milliseconds. The physics is real, so the limit is too. [Play it](https://pratyushshivam.github.io/latency/).

## Lookback

The universe at true scale. You start above Earth and scroll out, in one unbroken motion, to the edge of the observable universe. Every size and distance is real. [Open it](https://pratyushshivam.github.io/lookback/).

## Nine Zeros

One slider, from one user to a billion. The system redraws itself as you drag, and a readout shows what it would cost. [Open it](https://pratyushshivam.github.io/nine-zeros/).

## Likely

A real language model running in your browser, showing the odds it gives each next word before it picks one. [Open it](https://pratyushshivam.github.io/likely/).

## Unasked

The things your browser tells every website without being asked, written out in plain sentences. Nothing leaves your device. [Open it](https://pratyushshivam.github.io/unasked/).
